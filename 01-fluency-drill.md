# 01 — Fluency Drill

Targets: fillers (um, aa, uh, like, you know), speaking too fast,
sentence length, and sentence formulation under pressure.

## How to use

1. Load `00-system-setup.md` first.
2. Send: `FLUENCY DRILL` (no topic — the coach picks one).
3. Speak or type your answer. The coach evaluates.
4. Reply `next` to get a fresh, non-repeated topic.

## Prompt

```
Run a FLUENCY DRILL on me.

TOPIC SELECTION — YOU PICK. DO NOT ASK ME FOR A TOPIC.
- Maintain an internal "used topics" list for this entire chat.
  Never repeat a topic you have already used on me.
- Rotate randomly between these pools:
  A) Daily life (work, commute, food, family, hobbies, money,
     health, neighbors, weekends, holidays, friendships, technology
     in daily use, shopping, household routines, social media habits,
     small daily inconveniences).
  B) IELTS Speaking Part 1 style (home, studies, work, hometown,
     daily routine, weather, reading, music, sports, films, food,
     clothes, languages, pets).
  C) IELTS Speaking Part 2 style cue-card topics (describe a
     person / place / event / object / activity / achievement you
     remember; describe a time you helped someone; describe a change
     you would like to make; describe a skill you learned; describe
     a journey you took).
- Bias 70% toward daily life and Part 1, 30% toward Part 2. Skip
  Part 3 for this mode.
- The first time you speak, ask me to speak for 60–90 seconds on
  the topic. Give me 10 seconds of silence first. Then evaluate.

After I reply with my answer, evaluate and report:
1. Filler count — list every um, uh, aa, like, you know, basically.
   Give the count.
2. Average words per sentence. Target 12–18. Flag any sentence over 25.
3. Restarts, false starts, trailed-off endings. Count them.
4. Did I actually answer the question, or did I drift?
5. Weak / generic vocabulary. List 5 words I used that a Band 9
   speaker would replace, and give the replacement.

Then:
- Rewrite my answer in Band 9 style. Same ideas, tighter.
- Give me ONE 60-second redo drill to fix the biggest issue.
- End by prompting me to reply `next` for a fresh, non-repeated
  topic. Do not start the next topic yourself — wait for my `next`.

Do not start with praise. Start with the failure.
```

## Why the coach picks the topic

Random, non-repeated topics from a real-life pool:
- trains you on the *formulation* problem, not the *content* problem
  (you already know your own rehearsed stories).
- mirrors the IELTS test, where the topic is given to you cold.
- removes the "I have to think of something to say" friction that
  wastes the warmup minute.
