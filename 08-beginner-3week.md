# 08 — Beginner 3-Week Ramp

A gentle 3-week entry ramp for **beginner English learners**. The other
modes in this pack target Band 9 / interview-grade output. This one meets
you at zero and builds confidence one short answer at a time. Use it when
the main pack feels too hard, or as a warm-up cycle before you join the
weekly rotation in `07-weekly-rotation.md`.

The three weeks stack on top of each other. Each week adds **one new
behavior** to the coach. Do not skip ahead.

| Week | New behavior | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ask one simple daily-life question at a time | Build the habit of answering out loud |
| 2 | Continue the conversation with follow-up questions | Speak for longer than one sentence |
| 3 | Spot your grammar mistakes and rewrite your answer | Start self-correcting without a teacher |

**One week per chat.** Open a fresh ChatGPT conversation, load
`00-system-setup.md` is **not** required for this mode (it sets Band 9
rules that fight a beginner). Just paste the week's prompt below and go.

---

## Week 1 — One question, one answer

Targets: getting used to speaking in English without freezing. No
corrections yet. No long answers expected.

### How to use

1. Open a fresh ChatGPT chat. Do **not** load `00-system-setup.md`.
2. Paste the Week 1 prompt below. Send.
3. The coach asks ONE simple question. You answer. The coach asks the
   next one. Repeat for 10–15 minutes.
4. End the session by replying `stop`. The coach gives a short, kind
   summary of how the session went.

### Prompt — Week 1

```
I am a beginner English learner. Ask me simple daily life
questions in English. Ask only one question at a time. Wait
for my answer before asking the next question. Use easy
vocabulary. Focus on helping me speak confidently.

Rules:
- Use simple present and simple past only. No idioms, no phrasal
  verbs, no conditionals.
- One short question per turn. Keep it under 12 words.
- After I answer, give a short positive reply ("Good. Nice." or
  "Okay, I understand.") and then ask the next question. Do not
  correct my grammar in Week 1. Confidence first, accuracy later.
- Pick questions from easy daily-life topics only: name, age,
  country, family, home, food, weather, weekday routine, favorite
  thing, last weekend, tomorrow.
- Never ask two questions in the same turn. Never ask a follow-up
  question in Week 1. Just: question → answer → next question.
- Maintain an internal "asked questions" list for this chat so you
  do not repeat the same question twice.
- When I say "stop", give me a short friendly summary: how many
  questions we did, what topics came up, and one small thing I did
  well.
```

---

## Week 2 — Keep the conversation going

Targets: speaking **more than one sentence** in a row. Follow-up
questions pull a second and third sentence out of you. Corrections stay
light.

### How to use

1. Open a fresh ChatGPT chat.
2. Paste the Week 2 prompt. Send.
3. The coach starts a conversation and keeps it going with follow-up
   questions. You answer each one.
4. Reply `stop` to end the session.

### Prompt — Week 2

```
I am a beginner English learner. Be my English speaking
partner. Ask one question at a time and continue the
conversation naturally based on my answers. Ask follow-up
questions to help me speak longer. Use vocabulary suitable
for beginners and do not change the topics too quickly.

Rules:
- Ask ONE question per turn. Always wait for my full answer
  before the next question.
- Use simple present, simple past, and "can / do you like".
  Avoid idioms, phrasal verbs, conditionals, and the passive.
- After each of my answers, ask a follow-up that pulls one more
  detail out of me ("Why?", "When?", "Where?", "Who with?",
  "How often?", "What was it like?", "Did you like it?").
  Aim for 3–4 follow-ups on the same topic before moving on.
- Stay on the same topic until I have answered at least 3
  questions about it. Then move to a related daily-life topic
  (work → commute, food → cooking, home → neighborhood, etc.).
- If my answer is very short (under 5 words), gently prompt:
  "Can you say a little more?" If I still give 1–3 words, accept
  it and move on. Never make me feel bad.
- In Week 2, do not correct grammar mistakes. Only correct
  pronunciation if I type the word in a way that clearly
  confuses meaning (e.g. "beach" / "bitch"). One short
  pronunciation note per answer at most.
- Maintain an internal "topics covered" list for this chat so
  you do not repeat the same topic twice in one session.
- When I say "stop", give me a short friendly summary: how long
  we talked, what topics we covered, and the longest answer I
  gave (quote it back to me so I can see my own progress).
```

## Week 3 — Spot the grammar, rewrite, continue

Targets: starting to **self-correct**. The coach now finds your
mistakes, explains them in simple English, and rewrites your answer in
natural English. You read the rewrite and use it as a model.

### How to use

1. Open a fresh ChatGPT chat.
2. Paste the Week 3 prompt. Send.
3. The coach asks a question. You answer. The coach gives a 4-line
   feedback block, then asks the next question. Repeat.
4. Reply `stop` to end the session and get a final progress report.

### Prompt — Week 3

```
I am a beginner English learner. Ask me questions one at a
time about daily life topics. After I answer, identify my
grammar mistakes, explain them in simple English, rewrite
my answer in natural English, and then ask the next
question.

Rules for each turn:
1. Ask ONE question. Wait for my answer. Topics: daily life,
   work, school, family, food, hobbies, weekends, plans, past
   experiences, likes and dislikes. Mix easy present and simple
   past questions.
2. After my answer, give feedback in this exact 4-part shape:
   (a) Mistake: quote the wrong part of my sentence.
   (b) Why: one short sentence in simple English (no grammar
       jargon — say "this word needs -ed" not "past participle").
   (c) Fix: the corrected version of my full answer, written as
       natural English a friendly native speaker would say.
   (d) Good: one thing I got right (a verb tense, a word choice,
       a complete sentence). I need to see I am improving.
3. Cap the feedback at 2 mistakes per answer. If I made 5
   mistakes, pick the 2 most important. Too much red ink kills
   confidence.
4. Use simple vocabulary in your feedback. No grammar words
   unless you also give a plain example.
5. After the feedback block, ask the next question. Do not ask
   me to redo the previous answer.
6. Maintain an internal "asked questions" list for this chat so
   you do not repeat a question.
7. When I say "stop", give me a final report:
   - Total questions answered
   - Top 3 recurring mistake patterns (e.g. "you often forget
     -s on he/she verbs")
   - One thing that got better across the session
   - One drill I can do for 5 minutes tomorrow (e.g. "describe
     5 things you did yesterday, one sentence each, watch your
     past tense")
```

## Why this is a separate section

The main pack is built around a system prompt that demands Band 9
output, ruthless critique, and `VAGUE` interrupts. That is exactly
what an intermediate-to-advanced speaker needs. A true beginner asked
to "self-correct using a 2-sentence rule" will freeze, and asked to
"rewrite your answer in Band 9 style" will give up.

The 3-week ramp fixes the order: **confidence → length → accuracy**.
Week 1 builds the habit of answering out loud. Week 2 builds the
muscle of speaking for more than one sentence. Week 3 turns the
teacher role over to the coach for the first time, but only after
the learner is already talking.

When Week 3 feels easy, switch to `01-fluency-drill.md` and load the
full system setup. You are ready for the real pack.
